Epoxy grout is worth considering when resistance to staining, water and cleaning chemicals is a priority. A suitable cement-based grout can also be an option for a bathroom. Choose the exact product around your tile, joint dimensions and maintenance needs; neither choice replaces the waterproofing system or the flexible joints specified for the installation.

For a Canberra renovation, ask for two complete grout options before approving the tiling quote. Comparing only a colour name or the word “premium” leaves too much undecided.

## Compare the products, not just the labels

[Sika Australia's grout guidance](https://aus.sika.com/en/construction/tile-stone-installationsystems/tile-grouts.html) distinguishes the higher water and chemical resistance of epoxy from standard cementitious grout, while stressing compatibility with the complete installation. It also makes clear that epoxy does not remove the need for waterproofing.

| Decision | Cement-based option | Epoxy option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What to specify | Product name, colour and any approved additive | Product name, colour and complete component system |
| Tile and joint fit | Check the permitted joint range and tile finish | Check the permitted joint range and any special tile procedures |
| Appearance | Approve a dried sample with the chosen tile | Approve a cured sample with the chosen tile |
| Installation plan | Confirm mixing, finishing and drying conditions | Confirm mixing, clean-up and curing conditions |
| Quote comparison | Include preparation, joints and handover | Include the same scope before comparing the total |

These are questions for the proposed installation, not a claim that every product in either family performs alike.

## Check joint width and the tile face first

For a concrete example, ARDEX lists [FG 8](https://ardexaustralia.com/pdf/products/datasheets/tiling/ARDEX%20FG%208%20Datasheet.pdf) for 1 to 8 mm joints and [EG 15](https://ardexaustralia.com/product/ardex-eg-15/) for 1.5 to 15 mm joints. Those are product-specific ranges, not instructions to change your planned tile spacing.

EG 15's product information flags porous, moisture-sensitive and textured tiles for special application procedures. Bring the actual tile sample and its supplier details to the discussion. A display tile with a different finish is not a useful substitute.

Ask the tiler to demonstrate the proposed grout and clean-up on a spare tile or agreed sample area. Check it after curing in your bathroom lighting. Look at the grout colour, joint finish and tile surface together. The project photograph above illustrates finished tiled surfaces; it does not identify the grout formulation used.

## Keep flexible joints and waterproofing separate

A matching colour does not make grout and silicone interchangeable. ARDEX describes [SE silicone](https://ardexaustralia.com/product/ardex-se/) for movement joints, internal corners and connections to items such as baths and benchtops. It also excludes some natural stones, showing why the adjoining material matters when choosing sealant.

Have the quote identify which joints receive grout and which receive a compatible flexible sealant. Read our [layout and movement-joint guide](/en/guides/tile-layout-grout-movement-joints) alongside the [bathroom waterproofing checklist](/en/guides/bathroom-waterproofing-canberra).

If this is an existing shower with leakage, loose tiles or recurring cracks, request an assessment before selecting a replacement grout. Our [regrout, reseal or re-tile guide](/en/guides/regrout-reseal-or-retile) helps organise that conversation.

## Agree when the bathroom can return to use

Do not turn a product's “walkable” time into a promise that the shower is ready. The [EG 15 datasheet](https://ardexaustralia.com/pdf/products/datasheets/tiling/ARDEX%20EG%2015%20Datasheet.pdf) distinguishes light traffic from full cure and chemical resistance, and notes that lower temperatures delay curing. It also requires residue removal before hardening. FG 8's sheet separately notes that cooler or more humid conditions can lengthen drying.

Ask for written handover times for walking, showering, cleaning and any proposed sealing, based on all products used and actual site conditions. For an unheated Canberra bathroom, flag that condition before the work is scheduled. If it is your only bathroom, agree how the household will manage the period out of use.

## A quote checklist you can copy

Send these details with your enquiry:

- The tile manufacturer, product name, size and finish, with a photo or sample.
- Whether the work is a new installation or replacement of existing grout.
- The areas involved: shower floor, shower walls and the rest of the bathroom.
- The proposed joint width and grout colour, or a request for sample options.
- A request for each grout's full product name and the reason it suits that area.
- A separate description of flexible joints, preparation and final clean-up.
- Any single-bathroom access constraint, plus requested return-to-use instructions.
- A request to identify inclusions and exclusions before comparing prices.

Keep the product and colour record with your tile details after completion. For ongoing care, use the [tile and grout cleaning guide](/en/guides/tile-grout-cleaning-canberra) and the selected manufacturer's instructions; a chemical-resistance claim is not approval for every bathroom cleaner.

## Common questions

### Is epoxy always the better choice?

No single label decides the whole job. Ask what benefit the proposed product brings to your particular bathroom and whether the tile, joint and installation conditions suit it. Compare that benefit with the complete quoted scope.

### Will the grout colour exactly match the chart?

Use a sample rather than a screen or printed chart as the approval point. FG 8's manufacturer notes that lighting, site conditions and material characteristics can affect the appearance.

### Does choosing epoxy mean I can omit silicone?

No. Preserve joints designed to accommodate movement and specify a compatible sealant for their location and adjoining materials.
